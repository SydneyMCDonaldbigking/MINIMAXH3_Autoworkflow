# Classic Wagyu Sukiyaki — No-Person 3×5s Storyboard

Three independent 5-second H3 R2V clips. No people appear; only cropped anonymous hands may perform indispensable cooking actions.

## Clip 1: Wagyu and shallow-pan setup

- Locked 45mm table-height shot. Begin on the empty shallow cast-iron pan and separate irregular 1 mm Wagyu slices, tofu, napa cabbage, shiitake, enoki, long onion and shirataki around it. A small controlled stream of dark sweet-savoury sauce appears from just out of frame and stops in the pan. No person or hand is visible. End with a shallow glossy pool, not a full soup.
- Cut to a 70mm overhead insert. A few paper-thin Wagyu slices settle naturally into the shallow sauce, folded at their soft edges. They remain pink-red and translucent at the starting moment and no vegetables are moved. End as the slices begin to warm in the pan.
- Cut to a 65mm side close shot. The same 1 mm Wagyu slices develop delicate edge curls and a thin cooked sheen while the sauce clings in a shallow layer. No thick slabs or exaggerated white marbling. The burner flame stays stable. End with the beef ready for ingredients.

## Clip 2: Sweet-soy Wagyu sear

- Locked 55mm pan-height shot. Begin with the thin glossy Wagyu at one side of the shallow pan. Tofu and shiitake sit in the remaining sauce in clean distinct zones; no person or hand is visible.
- Cut to an 80mm close insert. Napa cabbage, long onion, enoki and shirataki settle in around the beef, then the sauce makes a gentle shallow simmer. No ingredient splashes or floats. End with the shiitake and fully cooked thin folded Wagyu surfaces glossy.
- Cut to a 65mm medium close shot. One small fully cooked Wagyu ribbon rests above the clear brown glaze on the pan edge, showing real delicate layers, chestnut-brown colour all the way through and a tender curl with no pink centre. End steady for the finishing hero.

## Clip 3: Glazed sukiyaki finish

- Locked 50mm three-quarter pan shot. Begin on settled thin Wagyu, tofu, mushrooms, napa cabbage, long onion and shirataki in shallow warishita. A small ladle adds one spoonful of sauce at the pan edge, then leaves the food untouched; no person or hand is visible.
- Cut to an 85mm macro texture insert. Steam lifts from the naturally folded thin Wagyu and shiitake while the reduced soy-mirin glaze catches warm light. Keep the sauce thin and clear, not cloudy or excessive. End on believable beef texture.
- Cut to a calm 60mm final hero. The pan stays still: a few loosely folded Wagyu ribbons sit in their sauce with vegetables and tofu in distinct sections. No utensil lift and no human visible. End on the complete classic sukiyaki pan.
