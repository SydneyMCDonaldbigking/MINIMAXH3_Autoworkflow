# Braised Eggplant with Minced Pork (肉末茄子)

## Production Use

This recipe bible is for a vertical food commercial built around Chinese
eggplant, browned minced pork, and a dark braise that collapses the eggplant to
glossy.

**The pork and the eggplant are never raw in the wok at the same time.** Our first
version tipped them in together, which is the one thing both routes agree cannot
happen. It also inherited a template fault that rendered the wok already full of
the finished dish while raw mince was still being added; that is fixed by
`vessel_start` in the dish config.

## Real Recipe Basis

Two routes exist and both reach the same place:

- **Pork first:** aromatics briefly, then the minced pork browned until no pink
  remains, then the cut eggplant, then the sauce, then a covered braise of about
  15 minutes until tender. This is the route the ad follows, because "browned
  crumb, then eggplant on top" reads clearly in five seconds.
- **Eggplant first:** the eggplant is cooked alone until the cut faces are lightly
  charred and it just begins to soften, about five minutes, then lifted out; the
  pork is browned in fresh oil; the eggplant returns for the sauce.

Common to both, and the detail that makes the dish work: the eggplant is **cubed
or cut into batons, soaked in salt water about 15 minutes, then squeezed dry.**
This makes it cook faster and absorb far less oil, so it drinks up sauce instead.

Minced meat must reach 75 C throughout - higher than a whole cut, because mincing
carries surface bacteria into the middle. The visible cue is no pink anywhere in
the crumb before the eggplant goes in.

## Recipe

Serves: 3

Ingredients:

- 3 Chinese eggplants, cut into thick batons
- 1 tbsp salt, for the soaking water
- 250 g minced pork
- 3 garlic cloves and 1 tsp ginger, chopped
- 2 whole dried chillies
- 2 spring onions, chopped
- Sauce: 1 tbsp light soy, 1 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp sugar, 150 ml stock,
  1 tsp corn starch

Method:

1. Cut the eggplant into thick batons. Soak in salted water about 15 minutes, then
   squeeze the water out. The cut faces should look matt and slightly compressed,
   not wet.
2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a bowl.
3. Heat oil in an empty wok. Add the minced pork **on its own**, spread it out,
   and brown it into a loose dry crumb with no pink left.
4. Add garlic, ginger and dried chilli and fry until fragrant.
5. Add the eggplant on top of the browned pork and turn it through.
6. Pour in the sauce, cover, and braise about 15 minutes over medium heat until
   the eggplant is tender and collapsing and the sauce has thickened and turned
   glossy.
7. Scatter spring onion and serve with rice.

## Visual Food States

- Product state: raw minced pork filling a plain black tray, coarse texture with
  visible white fat, **tray bare and unlabelled**. This reference was cropped from
  the two-tray product photo specifically to exclude the printed label.
- Prep state: eggplant batons in a colander, cut faces matt and slightly
  compressed from being salted and squeezed, and **a separate bowl of raw minced
  pork** beside them with chopped garlic, ginger, dried chilli, spring onion and a
  bowl of dark pre-mixed sauce.
- First heat: mince spread across **bare hot steel**, sizzling hard and colouring
  at the edges, fat rendering. Nothing else in the wok.
- Cook state: browned pork crumb at the bottom with eggplant batons added **on top
  of it**, cut faces turning translucent and golden as they take up the dark
  sauce, aromatics visible among them.
- Hero state: a shallow ceramic bowl, eggplant collapsed soft and glossy with dark
  sauce, skin still deep purple, browned pork clinging over and between the
  batons, spring onion on top, rice beside it.

## Commercial Beat Chain

Clip 01: product hook, the last water pressed out of the salted eggplant, the
eggplant and the pork kept apart, then the pork alone tipped into an **empty** hot
wok.

Clip 02: the pork finishes browning to a dry crumb with no pink left, the eggplant
goes in on top of it, and both collapse into the sauce as it thickens and clings.

Clip 03: tipped into the bowl with the thick sauce following, spring onion
scattered over the dark glaze, one soft baton lifted with chopsticks.
