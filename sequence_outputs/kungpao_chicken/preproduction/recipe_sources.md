# Recipe sources - Kung Pao chicken (宫保鸡丁), cucumber and carrot version

Researched 2026-08-11 for the UMALL / Asian Grocer Online Instagram ad. Facts are
paraphrased; no source text is reproduced at length.

**Client direction, 2026-08-11: this ad uses the home-style version with diced
cucumber and carrot.** That is the version sold to this market, and it is a real
and common variant, but it is not the Sichuan original. Both are researched
below because the technique comes from the classic and the ingredients from the
home-style.

## Recipe sources

1. **Red House Spice, "Kung Pao Chicken, the Authentic Way (宫保鸡丁)"**
   <https://redhousespice.com/kung-pao-chicken/>
   Technique facts taken: the chicken is cut into bite-sized cubes and velveted
   with corn starch, water and sesame oil, mixed until no liquid is visible. The
   sauce is **pre-mixed in a bowl before the wok is lit** - light and dark soy,
   black rice vinegar, Shaoxing wine, sugar, corn starch and water - and stirred
   again just before it goes in. Order in the wok: oil first, then **dried
   chillies and Sichuan peppercorns until they smell fragrant**, then the chicken
   for about 30 seconds, then scallion, garlic and ginger, then fry on until the
   chicken has completely lost its pink. The sauce goes in and **thickens in
   about 20 seconds**, at which point the heat comes off. **Peanuts go in last,
   off the heat, or they lose their crunch.**

2. **Home-style 宫保鸡丁 with cucumber and carrot**
   <https://simplechinesefood.com/recipe/kung-pao-chicken-240752>
   <https://cookpad.com/eng/recipes/24757539>
   Ingredient and order facts taken: carrot and cucumber are washed and diced to
   roughly the size of the chicken cubes, about 100 g each against 300 g of
   chicken. The chicken is marinated with salt, cooking wine, pepper and starch.
   After the aromatics, **carrot goes in first and is fried until it softens**,
   **then the cucumber**, which needs only a moment, then the chicken returns
   with the peanuts and the seasoning is stirred through. Cucumber last is the
   point: it is mostly water and turns limp and grey if it goes in early.

## Safety source

3. **NSW Food Authority / FSANZ, cooking temperatures**
   <https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-at-home/cooking-temperatures>
   Chicken must reach 75 C internally. The visual cue we can show is the cube
   surface turning from pink-translucent to opaque white-gold on every face
   before the sauce goes in. No shot may show a cut cube with pink at the centre.

## What this changes about our first version

The 2026-08-11 ad had the chicken hit the wok with dried chillies already
scattered around it, then "the sauce goes in and thickens rapidly, while peanuts
and dried chillies tumble among them". Four things are wrong:

- **no cucumber and no carrot at all** - the client's version is defined by them;
- the sauce was improvised into the wok rather than **pre-mixed**, which is how
  the vinegar-sugar balance is actually controlled;
- the chillies and Sichuan peppercorns were never **bloomed in the oil first**,
  which is where the dish's aroma comes from;
- **peanuts went in with the sauce**, which is precisely what every source warns
  against. They go in last, off the heat, or they go soft.
