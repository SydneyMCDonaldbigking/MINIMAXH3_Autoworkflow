# Recipe sources - Thai volcano pork ribs (泰式火山排骨 / Leng Saap, เล้งแซ่บ)

Researched 2026-08-11 for the UMALL / Asian Grocer Online Instagram ad. Facts are
paraphrased; no source text is reproduced at length.

## Recipe sources

1. **Cookidoo (Thermomix official), "Thai Volcano Spare Ribs"**
   <https://cookidoo.co.uk/recipes/recipe/en-GB/r928486>
   The method is behind a membership wall, but the ingredient structure is public
   and is itself decisive. The ribs sit under a heading called **"Stewed pork
   ribs"**, with roughly 900-1000 g of baby ribs about 10 cm long against 2900 g
   of **water**, plus sliced ginger, bruised spring onion, bruised lemongrass,
   coriander root and salt. A separate **"Sauce and assembly"** section lists
   chillies, garlic, sugar, lime juice, fish sauce, shallots, spring onion and
   coriander leaves - no oil, no heat, no thickener.

2. **Leng Saap / volcano rib demonstrations** (Bangkok street-food dish)
   <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U3aARtSu5o0>
   <https://www.instagram.com/missssaucy/reel/C4SPCtoP7Mv/>
   Corroborate the same shape: the ribs are rubbed with corn starch and salt and
   soaked in water for about 20 minutes to draw out blood, then started in **cold
   water** with the aromatics, brought to the boil and simmered covered on medium
   heat for **at least an hour** until the meat pulls back from the bone. They are
   then stacked upright into the cone that gives the dish its name, and the
   blended dressing is poured **from the top down** over the finished stack.

## Safety source

3. **NSW Food Authority / FSANZ, cooking temperatures**
   <https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-at-home/cooking-temperatures>
   Pork should reach 75 C internally. An hour of covered simmering clears this
   comfortably; the visual cue we can show is meat visibly shrinking back from
   the bone ends and a fork going in without resistance.

## What this changes about our first version

The 2026-08-11 ad was **the wrong dish**. It showed marinated ribs dropped into a
wok of hot oil, deep fried to a dark crust, and stacked. Leng Saap is not fried
at all:

- the ribs are **simmered in water** with lemongrass, ginger, spring onion and
  coriander root for about an hour, so they come out pale, tender and steaming,
  not dark and crusted;
- the "volcano" is the **stack**, not the frying;
- the sauce is **raw** - chilli, garlic, lime juice, fish sauce, sugar, shallot,
  coriander, blended and poured over at the end. It is never cooked, and it is
  what makes the dish red, not caramelisation.

Nothing about the original clip 02 survives. It has to be rebuilt around a
covered pot and a long simmer, and clip 03 around the stack and the pour.
