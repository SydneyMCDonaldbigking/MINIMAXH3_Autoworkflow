# Recipe sources - salt and pepper prawns (椒盐虾)

Researched 2026-08-11 for the UMALL / Asian Grocer Online Instagram ad. Facts are
paraphrased; no source text is reproduced at length.

## Recipe sources

1. **Red House Spice, "Salt and Pepper Shrimp (椒盐虾)"**
   <https://redhousespice.com/salt-and-pepper-shrimp/>
   Production facts taken: shell stays on and the prawns are patted dry with
   kitchen paper before anything else; they are tossed in corn starch until
   evenly coated; deep fried at 190 C for about 90 seconds, or shallow fried
   until the first side turns pink; removed when pink and visibly crisp, because
   overcooking costs the tenderness of the flesh. The frying oil is then poured
   off to leave only a thin layer; shallots, chilli, garlic and ginger fry in
   that thin oil over medium heat for about a minute; only then do the fried
   prawns go back in, with the salt-and-pepper seasoning, and get tossed.
   Finished with scallions and served immediately while still crisp.

2. **Christine's Recipes, "Salt and Pepper Prawns"**
   <https://en.christinesrecipes.com/2009/09/salt-and-pepper-prawns.html>
   Corroborates: prawns trimmed and deveined, rinsed, drained and patted dry;
   coated with corn flour with the excess shaken off; deep fried in batches; done
   when the prawn turns red-pink and curls; the salt and pepper mixture is
   sprinkled and tossed at the end, not cooked with the prawns.

   Also corroborated by The Woks of Life and Curated Kitchenware / Souped Up
   Recipes, which describe the same "jiao yan" pattern: fry until crisp first,
   season afterwards. The pepper in the dish name is Sichuan peppercorn, and the
   salt is dry-roasted before being mixed with it.

## Safety source

3. **NSW Food Authority, cooking temperatures** (FSANZ-aligned)
   <https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/food-at-home/cooking-temperatures>
   Prawns and other seafood are done when the flesh is firm and has turned from
   translucent to opaque. A thermometer is the only reliable check for meat, but
   for prawns the visual cue is the accepted one. Nothing in this ad may show a
   prawn served translucent or grey.

## What this changes about our first version

The 2026-08-11 ad put raw prawns straight into a wok with garlic and chilli
already in it, and plated them from there. That skips the two things the dish is
named for:

- there is **no starch coating and no crisp fry** - salt and pepper prawns are a
  deep-fried dish, and the crust is the point;
- the aromatics and the seasoning go in **after** the prawns come out of the oil,
  in the drained wok, not alongside raw prawns.

The corrected clip structure follows: coat and fry, drain and bloom aromatics,
return and toss.
