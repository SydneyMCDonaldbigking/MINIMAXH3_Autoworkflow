# Cantonese Salt and Pepper Prawns (椒盐虾)

## Production Use

This recipe bible is for a vertical food commercial built around whole shell-on
prawns, a starch-dusted crisp fry, and a dry salt-and-pepper toss.

**The dish is named for its last step, not its first.** "Jiao yan" means the food
is fried crisp and *then* seasoned. Our first version put raw prawns into a wok
that already held garlic and chilli and cooked everything together, which produces
a wet stir-fry, not this dish.

## Real Recipe Basis

- Prawns stay **shell on**. The shell is what goes crisp and is meant to be eaten.
- They are rinsed and **patted completely dry**, then tossed in corn starch until
  evenly dusted, with the excess shaken off. Wet prawns will not crust.
- Deep fried at **190 C for about 90 seconds**, or shallow fried until the first
  side turns pink. Out as soon as they are pink and crisp; longer costs tenderness.
- The oil is then **poured off to a thin film**. Shallots, chilli, garlic and
  ginger fry in what remains for about a minute.
- The fried prawns go **back in** and are tossed with the salt and pepper mixture.
- The salt is dry-roasted before use and mixed with ground Sichuan peppercorn -
  the "pepper" in the name is Sichuan pepper, not black pepper.
- Served immediately, while the shells are still crisp.

Seafood doneness is visual: prawns are done when the flesh is firm and has turned
from translucent to opaque.

## Recipe

Serves: 2-3

Ingredients:

- 500 g whole prawns, shell on
- 3 tbsp corn starch
- Neutral oil for frying
- 4 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 thumb ginger, shredded
- 2 red chillies, sliced
- 2 spring onions, cut into lengths
- 1 tsp sea salt, dry roasted
- 2 tsp Sichuan peppercorn, ground
- Lemon wedge, to serve

Method:

1. Dry roast the salt in a dry pan until it turns faintly yellow. Cool and mix
   with the ground Sichuan pepper. The usual balance is about two parts pepper to
   one of salt.
2. Rinse the prawns and pat them completely dry with kitchen paper.
3. Toss them through the corn starch until every shell carries a thin even
   dusting. Shake off the excess.
4. Heat the oil to 190 C. Fry the prawns about 90 seconds, until the shells go
   bright orange-red and look dry and blistered. Lift out and drain.
5. Pour off the oil, leaving only a thin film. Fry the garlic, ginger, chilli and
   spring onion over medium heat about a minute, until fragrant.
6. Return the prawns, add the salt and pepper mixture, and toss once so it coats
   every shell.
7. Serve immediately with a lemon wedge, while still crisp.

## Visual Food States

- Product state: raw whole prawns, translucent grey shells, no packaging in frame.
- Prep state: prawns in a shallow bowl under an even white starch dusting, beside
  separate small piles of chopped garlic, shredded ginger, sliced chilli, cut
  spring onion, and a dish of roasted salt with Sichuan pepper.
- Fry state: a wok of **clear** hot oil, prawns turning from translucent grey
  through pink to bright orange-red, shells blistering and crisping, bodies
  curling. **No aromatics and no seasoning in the oil at this stage** - this is
  the single most important frame in the ad.
- Drain state: a wire spider holding the whole batch clear of the oil, oil running
  back in fine threads, shells dry-looking and crisp.
- Toss state: the drained wok with a thin film of oil and just-fried garlic,
  chilli, ginger and spring onion, prawns tipped back in and turned once.
- Hero state: prawns piled on a white platter, shells bright orange-red and
  visibly dry rather than saucy, crusted with coarse salt and pepper, studded with
  fried garlic and chilli, lemon at the edge.

## Commercial Beat Chain

Clip 01: product hook, prawns turned through corn starch and the excess shaken
off, then tipped into oil that is already at temperature and holds nothing else.

Clip 02: the prawns fry alone in clear oil, shells go orange and the crust sets,
a wire spider lifts the whole batch clear to drain.

Clip 03: aromatics bloom in the drained wok, the prawns come back and are tossed,
salt and pepper scattered dry over dry shells, one prawn lifted by the tail.
